All the tools you need.

No extra modules or hidden costs—Stata provides a complete suite of tools for reproducible statistical analysis, intuitive data visualization, efficient data management, and automated reporting.

Statistical features

Stata's statistical features empower you to answer a wide range of research questions. From linear and logistic regression to time-series and panel-data analyses, survival models, causal inference, Bayesian analysis, and machine learning, you can fit models, evaluate assumptions, make inferences, and interpret results with confidence.

Explore statistical features →

Publication-quality graphics

Create publication-quality graphs with customizable layouts, labels, and colors. Export seamlessly to EPS, PNG, PDF, SVG, or TIFF, making it easy to integrate visuals into reports and presentations.

Explore visualization features →

Prepare your data with confidence

Clean, reshape, and manage your data with ease. Stata's tools handle large datasets, missing values, merges, and time-series structures, helping you prepare your data for analysis using just a few commands.

See data management tools →

Automated reporting

Automate report generation with seamless exports to Word, Excel, PDF, and HTML. Create customizable tables and dynamic reports that update as your data change—ensuring transparency and reproducibility.

Automate your reporting →

Spotlight

A footballer ensemble of decision trees

Curious about Stata’s new machine learning features powered by H2O? Follow along with this example using gradient boosting machine and random forest to predict market value of soccer players. Do minutes played or goals scored have more impact?

Spotlight

Group sequential designs for clinical trials

Designing a clinical trial? Discover the gs suite of commands for calculating stopping boundaries and sample sizes for interim analyses or "looks". Stop the trial early for treatment efficacy or futility while controlling the familywise error rate.

Spotlight

Select predictors like a Bayesian—with probability

You want to know which of many potential regressors are most predictive, but you also want to interpret the results. Introducing bayesselect, a new command that performs Bayesian variable selection for linear regression. Simultaneously evaluate variable importance and estimate regression coefficients, and then make predictions.
